Reading and Written Response #3: “Graphic Novel: Motherhood in Crisis”

These four stories regarding the four women and their individual experiences of birth and motherhood really opened my eyes regarding the vast amount of hardships present all across the world. Where pregnancy, often referred to as a ceremony and a celebratory act of life, can be the complete opposite when it comes to complications. Many factors come into play during pregnancy and childbirth that I myself haven't really thought about as much as I did when reading these stories. In addition to this, the multiple factors in each woman's personal journey of childbirth and motherhood further highlighted issues I failed to even notice regarding the dangers of pregnancy in the first place. From the limited healthcare access in countries like Sierra Leone, to the cultural pressures, and poverty many families face when having a child, these pregnancy traditions and celebratory acts I understood, have another often not talked side many fail to understand. Before viewing these stories I always viewed the act of pregnancy as a celebration, as many common US pregnancy traditions focus on the act of celebrating through baby showers, gender reveals, and social media posts. Yet, these stories make the viewer see this harsh reality without a filtered lens allowing them to not only better understand the women's act of resilience and determination to protect their lives and their children, but also highlight broader structural problems in these countries that affect whole families. All four stories show the grueling reality women in Sierra Leone are faced with. All these stories not only show readers their individual situations but also provide their individual thoughts and actions showing their resilience and bravery given the horrible predicaments they are up against. While all the stories are equally depressing and sad, the story that really hit home for me was Heela – “My Stolen Future”. The situation Heela was faced with was horrible to say the least, as her future and dream was essentially destroyed due to her becoming pregnant to a man who not only sexually assaulted her but also refused to take responsibility for his actions. In addition to her family situation, already being in poverty, and the feelings of shame and isolation that comes with losing not only your dreams but ambitions, Heela, as with the three other women in these stories, are beyond brave and resilient. If I were to put myself in Heelas shoes I probably wouldn't have been as brave as her. She not only had the courage to speak out and seek help given the amount of shame and embarrassment she must have felt, but also decided to live for the sake of her child, to cast aside her dreams and ambition to better support her child's future. All four stories really provided me with that much needed view because it not only made me understand the sacrifices women make regarding motherhood but also in the cases of Memunatu, Heela, Kadiatu, and Tetteh, pregnancy which I once viewed as a celebratory act of life can be easily turned into a crisis due to factors like poverty, limited healthcare access, and systemic inequality .
